Most people think focus is a personality trait. Some of us have it, some of us don't. That's not quite right.

Focus is closer to a **skill you can design for**. Change your environment and your habits, and focus becomes the path of least resistance instead of something you have to fight for every single time.

Here are five small habits that make the biggest difference.

## 1. Protect the first hour

Whatever you do in the first hour after waking up sets the tone for the rest of your day. Open a screen time app before you open your inbox and you've already lost the morning.

## 2. Use a single, obvious timer

A visible countdown does more than any amount of self-discipline. When you can *see* the time running out, your brain treats the task differently.

> "You don't rise to the level of your goals, you fall to the level of your systems."

## 3. Batch your notifications

Notifications are the single biggest source of context-switching. Try batching them into two or three windows a day instead of reacting in real time.

## 4. Make distractions slightly harder

You don't need to delete every app. You just need enough friction that opening it is a decision, not a reflex.

- Move distracting apps off your home screen
- Turn off badges and previews
- Log out of anything you check compulsively

## 5. Review your week, not your day

Daily reviews are noisy. A short weekly review of your screen time trends tells you far more about what's actually working.

**Small changes, repeated consistently, beat big changes you can't sustain.**
